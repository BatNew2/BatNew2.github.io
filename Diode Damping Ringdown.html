Diode Damping Ringdown

Both circuits get the same sudden voltage step and ring at about 32 kHz. The diodes take a near-fixed bite out of the amplitude every half-cycle, so the decay is roughly a straight line. The resistor takes a fixed fraction, so the decay is exponential. Change the step size and watch which one cares.

Decay rate against step size

α₁ = (2/T)·ln(V0/A1), from the first overshoot. The resistor is chosen so both circuits match at 10 V, which is why the lines cross there.

What to watch

  1. Dashed envelopes. The diode peaks fall along a nearly straight line; the resistor peaks follow a curve that flattens out.
  2. Amplitude lost per half-cycle. For the diodes it stays above about 1.2 V (twice the diode drop) even when the swing is small. For the resistor it shrinks in proportion to the swing.
  3. Small steps. Drop V0 to 2–4 V. The diode circuit barely completes a cycle, while the resistor rings exactly as before, only smaller.
  4. Where it stops. The diode circuit freezes slightly off zero, inside the diodes' dead band, the same way a block with friction stops short of its rest position.
